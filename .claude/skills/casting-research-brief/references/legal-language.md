## Why this matters

The single largest legal risk for a research-brief feature is that a user (or a regulator, or a plaintiff) treats it as a substitute for an FCRA-compliant background check. The Fair Credit Reporting Act, plus parallel state statutes (CA ICRAA, NY FCRA, etc), regulates "consumer reports" used for employment, housing, credit, or insurance decisions. Penalties include statutory damages per violation, class-action exposure, and FTC consent decrees.

Mythie avoids becoming a CRA by:

1. **Not selling to make eligibility decisions.** The product copy explicitly directs users to regulated CRAs for those.
2. **Limiting to publicly-available information.** No paid people-data aggregators, no criminal records, no credit, no DMV, no sex-offender registries.
3. **Transparency to the subject.** The talent (subject) is notified each time a brief is run on them and can opt out.
4. **No adverse-action workflow.** Briefs do not include a "deny / approve" verdict - they aggregate signals; humans interpret.
5. **Locked disclaimers.** The product never describes itself in CRA-coded language.

## Banned product language

The following terms are **never** used in product copy, marketing copy, support docs, or admin UI to describe this feature:

- "Background check"
- "Background screening"
- "Vetting"
- "Verified" (as a standalone badge)
- "Cleared"
- "Approved" / "Not approved" (as a verdict)
- "Criminal record check"
- "Pass" / "Fail"
- "Risk score" (use "confidence" or "signal strength")
- "Screening report"
- "Eligibility"
- "Trustworthy" / "Untrustworthy" (when applied to the subject)

## Approved product language

- **Feature name (any of):** "Talent Insights", "Pre-Casting Research", "Public Footprint Brief", "Research Brief".
- **Action verbs:** "research", "look up", "review public information", "gather public signals", "generate a brief".
- **Quality descriptors:** "confidence" (high/medium/low), "signal strength", "match quality", "public mention".
- **Outcome descriptors:** "ready", "insufficient public signal", "see partner check for a regulated background check".

## The locked banner

This banner is rendered at the top of every brief view, in print exports, and in any email that references a brief. Text:

> **Public-internet research only.** This is not a background check, consumer report, or screening tool. The information here was gathered from publicly available sources and may be incomplete, outdated, or refer to a different individual. For employment, housing, credit, or insurance decisions, use a regulated background check. [Run a regulated check →]

The "[Run a regulated check →]" link routes to the partner-CRA explainer.

## The talent-side ToS clause (Mythie sign-up)

A new sign-up consent clause for talents, version v1-2026-05:

> **Public-research permission.** Mythie may, at the request of a casting team viewing your profile, generate a research brief summarising publicly available information about you (such as press mentions, public social profiles, and public court-records references). This is not a background check. You will be notified each time a brief is generated, and you may opt out of research at any time in your Privacy settings. Opting out will reduce visibility of your profile to some casting teams. Mythie will never share your contact details with a casting team without your consent.

The talent can toggle `subjectTalent.privacy.allowResearch` to `false` at any time, which immediately blocks new brief generation on that profile (existing cached briefs are purged within 24h).

## The casting-team ToS clause (Mythie casting workspace)

A new admin-acceptance clause, version v1-2026-05, accepted at the casting workspace level (not per-user):

> **Research Brief acceptable use.** Research briefs generated by Mythie are public-internet research products and are **not** consumer reports under the FCRA or any state equivalent. You may not use a Research Brief to make any employment, housing, credit, or insurance eligibility decision, in whole or in part. For such decisions, you must obtain a regulated background check from a licensed consumer reporting agency. You may not export, resell, or republish briefs. You may not generate briefs to harass, stalk, or target individuals. Violations may result in account termination and may be reported to law enforcement.

## Jurisdiction watchlist

Per `legal-compliance-guardian/references/jurisdiction-watchlist.md`:

- **California (ICRAA, CCRAA):** Strictest US-state ICRA. The "public records" exemption is narrow. We rely on the federal FCRA public-record exemption and the no-eligibility-decision posture; review on every CA AG enforcement update.
- **New York:** "Stop Credit Discrimination in Employment Act" + Fair Chance Act. Same posture.
- **Illinois (BIPA + IL Personnel Record Review Act):** No biometric data ever in briefs.
- **EU/UK GDPR:** Subject access rights apply. Talents based in EU/UK can request export and erasure of any brief about them. `researchBriefs` schema's TTL purge supports this; export endpoint required (defer to v1.1, manual fulfilment until then).
- **Quebec (Law 25):** Similar to GDPR; same posture.

## Defamation posture

A brief that misattributes a court record or news article to the wrong person is a defamation risk. Mitigations:

1. **Identity-match threshold** - signals < 0.80 confidence appear behind a "possible match - verify" expand, with the wording "possibly the same person".
2. **Cite the source on every claim** - the AI summary references a signal ID for every assertion; the signal ID links to the originating URL.
3. **30-day TTL** - no permanent dossier exists for a talent to sue over.
4. **Subject notification** - the subject can see what was surfaced about them and request takedown.
5. **No re-publication** - the casting-team ToS prohibits export/resale; the UI omits a "share" button.

## Logging & audit

Every brief lifecycle event is logged immutably to `researchBriefAuditLog`. Retention: 6 years (matches FCRA records-retention norms, even though we are not a CRA, this is the cheapest defensible posture). This collection has `default-deny + admin-read-only` rules and is never exposed to casting teams.
